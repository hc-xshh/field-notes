Most writing about AI tooling is written by people who watched a demo. This site is written
by someone who runs the same task book against five tools, then reads the 39 acceptance
checks line by line to find out which ones passed for the wrong reason.

That difference is the whole point, and it comes with a cost: it is slower. So the rules here.

**One long post every two weeks, not three short ones a week.** Each post has to contain
something a reader can re-run, copy, or argue against — a task book, a scoring rule, a
failure taxonomy, the script that computed the number.

**Numbers come from scripts, not from impressions.** If a post says a tool passed 39 out of 39,
there is a script that produced that 39, and I will say what it could not see. Where I have not
verified something, the post says so in those words.

**No employer internals.** The method is mine to publish; the data is not. Anything under an
NDA, any customer ticket, and any internal number stays out.

**If it breaks on the third run, the post says so.** The failure mode is usually the most
useful sentence in the whole piece.

If that sounds like the kind of thing you want in your inbox, subscribe when the signup form
opens — until then, the [posts](/posts/) page is where everything lands.
