Stand in a park here before breakfast and you will see the same eight movements, done at the same
speed, to a recording that counts the breaths out loud. It takes about twelve minutes. It looks
simple from outside, which is why so many get the shapes wrong.

What follows is what each section asks of the body, which direction things travel, and the mistakes
the sport administration's own correction sheet names.

**This is not medical advice.** It diagnoses and treats nothing; it describes a public routine and its
published instructions.

## Twelve minutes, in a table

The counts are in the standard. What people get wrong is that it is not eight movements, one round
each: section one is six rounds, section eight is seven.

| 0:38–2:10 | 1. 两手托天理三焦 | 6 | natural 1–2, in 3–4, brief hold 5, out 6 | 93 s |
| 2:10–3:39 | 2. 左右开弓似射雕 | 3 | in 1–2 and 4, out 3 and 5, hold as the bow sets | 89 s |

One up-and-down is one round for sections 1, 6 and 8; one left-and-right is one round for 2, 3, 4,
5 and 7 ([八段锦 团体标准, draft for comment §5](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5402/c29596700/part/29596710.pdf), April 2026).
The breathing column is the draft's own instruction
([§5.1–5.10](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5402/c29596700/part/29596710.pdf), April 2026).

"Twelve minutes" is a label, not a fixed length. Four public run-throughs whose titles all say twelve
minutes measure 708, 712, 719 and 735 seconds — 11:48 to 12:15
([708](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1gxr6YCEkn/),
[712](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1RtSXYMEdj/),
[719](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1VsDpYXEqD/),
[735](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1pSBeBhEpQ/), durations read from the platform pages, 2026-10-09).

The seconds above come from one run-through of 719 seconds. A second, independent pass over the
same recording agrees within a few seconds everywhere except two boundaries: section two starts
about eight seconds earlier in it, section seven about five seconds later. Treat the middle of the
table as ±10 seconds; the round counts, from the standard, are exact.

## The eight movements, one by one

The left column is position and direction, condensed from the standard's technical section; the
right column is what the sport administration's correction sheet names as the habitual error
([健身气功·八段锦习惯性错误及纠正方法](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5407/c843016/content.html), 10 January 2018).

| Section | Position and direction | Errors the correction sheet names |
|---|---|---|
| 预备势 | Feet together, then the left foot steps out to shoulder width, toes forward, weight between both legs. Arms rise to about 45° at the sides, palms back, then gather in front of the abdomen: palms inward at navel height, fingertips 10–20 cm apart, elbows dropped, armpits hollow, crown lifted. | Stepping out while watching the foot, or landing with the feet turned out — land the toes first, then press the heel out until the feet are parallel. Arms stiff and held too high with the fingers angled at the floor — keep the elbows down and the palms at navel height. |
| 1. 两手托天理三焦 | Fingers interlaced in front of the lower belly, palms up. The hands rise close to the body's vertical line and finish straight, beside the ears, with the chin tucked and the toes gripping the floor; the head goes back only as they pass the face and comes level once the arms are straight. On the way down they separate to about 45° below shoulder height, shoulders sinking, wrists relaxing. | Looking up before the hands rise — the eyes follow them from chest height. Arms leaning forward instead of travelling a near-vertical line. Ripping the hands apart at the top instead of softening the elbows and pressing the wrists so they open slowly. |
| 2. 左右开弓似射雕 | Wrists crossed about 30 cm from the chest at shoulder height, left arm outside. Push left at shoulder height with a flat hand, the middle, ring and little fingers bent only at the first two joints; pull right to the front of the right shoulder with the thumb tucked. The pushing wrist and the pulling elbow stay level with the shoulders; knees behind the toes; the head turns along the push while the trunk stays square. The round ends with the pulling hand opening up past the head and out to shoulder height, a half circle of about 180°. | Hands crossed too high and close. Weight drifting onto the pulling side, tilting the stance and dragging the pushing hand high. Opening the pulling hand straight forward instead of up and over. |
| 3. 调理脾胃须单举 | The raised palm flat and facing straight up, its middle fingertip on a vertical line over the shoulder that grazes the ear. The other hand presses down beside the hip, about 10 cm out, fingers forward, force in the heel of the palm. The big toe presses the floor as the two hands pull apart, and the raised arm returns by the path it went up. | The lifting hand ending forward of or outside the shoulder. The palm tilting instead of lying flat and facing up. The middle fingertip crossing the body's centre line on the way up or down — it passes the line, it does not cross it. |
| 4. 五劳七伤往后瞧 | Arms straight and slightly back, rotating out to about 45° from vertical with the palms facing diagonally back and up, shoulder blades drawing together as the chest opens; the hands never swing behind the body. The head turns without the trunk turning, chin tucked so both eyes stay on one level; the head returns before the hands press down beside the hips. | The arms barely rotating, so they stay beside the body rather than opening to 45°. The chin jutting up and the trunk leaning back, leaving the eyes at different heights. |
| 5. 摇头摆尾去心火 | Feet three foot-widths apart in a horse stance, fingertips on the inner thighs with the base of each palm hanging off the outside, so the hands carry nothing. The trunk tilts about 45°, bends forward and turns until the eyes are on the foot on that side; then the tailbone travels right, front, left and back while the head turns left and back, head higher than tail, each through roughly 270° in opposite directions. | Leaning on the palms. Bending too far with the hips pushed out, so head and tail move at one height. The tailbone swinging only backward, never forward. A stiff neck and lower back, so the trunk rocks and the shoulders wobble instead. |
| 6. 两手攀足固肾腰 | Palms press down in front of the chest with the hands low and the elbows high, near shoulder height, then thread back under the armpits and run down beside the spine; the trunk stays upright until the hands pass the buttocks. Knees straight for the whole descent. To rise: sink the lower back, lift the hips, raise the head, and let the arms lead the body up to horizontal and then overhead, hands beside the ears. | The hands sliding in too high under the armpits. Bending forward too early, or looking down as the hands run along the back. Standing up before the arms move. |
| 7. 攒拳怒目增气力 | Horse stance, fists at the waist with the fist eyes up. The fist closes gradually as the elbow leaves the ribs; the elbow is never locked; the toes grip and the eyes widen as they follow the fist. The waist turns with the shoulder following while the hips and head stay still. The palm traces a full circle around the wrist — down, right, up, left — then the thumb goes to the base of the ring finger and all four fingers fold over it together. | A fast punch with the elbow locked straight. An incomplete wrist rotation before the fingers close. Closing the fingers one at a time, starting from the little finger. |
| 8. 背后七颠百病消 | Feet together, crown lifted, shoulders down, tailbone tucked, abdomen drawn in. The heels rise with the toes gripping, legs straight and pressed together, the little toe taking the load at the outer edge. The weight stays between the middle of the foot and the ball, and the heels travel down evenly to tap, the teeth closing at the moment of contact. | The weight swinging forward and back, so the balance wobbles. A pause or a feeble landing on the way down — too hard and too light are both wrong. |
| 收势 | Arms out to about 45°, then in to gather over the lower belly with the palms stacked, one settling breath there, one more as the hands separate, one more before walking away. | Rushing the close — the correction sheet names impatience as the error. |

Four faults recur: the shoulders lift when they should sink; the trunk loses its upright line, usually
because a movement is taken further than described; the head drops or tilts, breaking the eye line
that several sections use as their reference; and in the held positions the hands travel past the
line they are given. Three positions are self-checking, which is why the text states them so
precisely: the knee behind the toe in the horse stance, the middle fingertip on the shoulder line in
section three, and the arms leading the rise in section six.

## How to tell you are doing it right

The standard gives a dose: at least three days a week, at least thirty minutes a day
([draft §6.3.3](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5402/c29596700/part/29596710.pdf), April 2026). Press
coverage of the release quotes a doctor who worked on the draft: 9–10 a.m. is the best time, and one
or two full run-throughs a day is enough
([人民网, 10 June 2026](http://health.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0610/c14739-40737848.html)).

Warm hands and feet, a comfortable mood, a clear head, a better appetite: the draft lists these as
normal, along with heat, cold, numbness, itching, feelings of being light, heavy, large or small,
crawling sensations and muscle twitching
([§6.4.2](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5402/c29596700/part/29596710.pdf), April 2026). Soreness
after a heavier session is handled by changing the load
([§6.4.1](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5402/c29596700/part/29596710.pdf), April 2026).

Older official material states the load test plainly: afterwards you should not feel tired, your
mood good, your pulse and blood pressure steady, appetite and sleep normal; if not, the load is too
high ([健身气功·八段锦"三调"要义](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5407/c838019/content.html), 5 December 2017).

On breathing, the table asks for a short hold at each held position; the 2017 article puts that hold
at about two seconds, but that is not where a beginner starts. Both it and the teachers interviewed
in the press say to learn the movements first on natural breathing and add the breath later
([新京报, 19 December 2023](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1702978392168256.html)).

## When to stop

Stop at once, rest, and get medical help if needed, if you get: chest tightness, pain in the front
of the chest, palpitations, a badly irregular heartbeat; dizziness, headache, blurred vision or a
blackout; difficulty breathing or severe shortness of breath; sharp joint or muscle pain; any other
clearly unbearable discomfort
([draft §8.2](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5402/c29596700/part/29596710.pdf), April 2026).

Do not force the breath to match the movement, and pause to ask someone qualified if anything odd
happens physically or mentally
([§8.1, §6.4.3](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5402/c29596700/part/29596710.pdf), April 2026). People
with existing conditions, or who are pregnant or menstruating, should be assessed by a doctor or
rehabilitation professional first, and the standard advises against it for people with severe mental
disorders ([§8.3](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5402/c29596700/part/29596710.pdf), April 2026).
A Beijing Sport University associate professor quoted in the press adds: not straight after a meal,
not after drinking, not while very upset
([新京报, 19 December 2023](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1702978392168256.html)); the draft says the
same in its own words, adding hunger and exhaustion.

The January 2026 briefing from the national traditional-medicine administration is the most specific
public statement I found on what to do short of stopping
([国家中医药管理局, 21 January 2026](http://www.natcm.gov.cn/bangongshi/gongzuodongtai/2026-01-21/37583.html)).
Fatigue lasting more than three days, or soreness bad enough to interfere with daily activity, or
soreness with dizziness, palpitations, a dry mouth or insomnia, is not the body adapting; it is a
method problem. The advice is to stop and find which part went wrong: too much range, so reduce the
range; wrong breathing, so drop the pairing for now and go back to plain abdominal breathing; too
much intensity, so shorten the session, for example from thirty minutes to fifteen, rest two or
three days and restart low and slow. The same briefing says older adults and people with high blood
pressure should exhale as soon as the inhale is full, not hold it.

For scale: a systematic review of 47 trials with 3,877 participants found that only two of them
reported any adverse event, and only 22 described how they checked for harm
([The Safety of Baduanjin Exercise, 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7847359/), 21 January 2021) —
rarely reported, not proven rare.

## Where the versions disagree

The order most people learn comes from the sport administration's set, maintained since 2003
([功法源流](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5407/c781297/content.html), 20 December 2016): lift, draw
the bow, one hand up, look back, shake head and tail, hold the feet, fists and stare, seven bounces.
That is the order the table measures and the standard fixes
([T/CACM 1676-2026, released 28 May 2026](https://ndls.cnis.ac.cn/standard/detail/8eaeb320d1881b670d7b0f8a97dab8c3)).
That version was compiled from 64 traditional versions and released in 2003
([国家中医药管理局, 21 January 2026](http://www.natcm.gov.cn/bangongshi/gongzuodongtai/2026-01-21/37583.html)),
and a Beijing Sport University professor told a newspaper in 2023 that more than seventy
circulate ([新京报, 19 December 2023](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1702978392168256.html)).

The older written tradition differs. The same page quotes the late-Qing illustrated version, the
first with the name and pictures, as: shake head and tail, **the seven bounces**, fists and stare,
**holding the feet**. Two positions swap, and a university museum page from 2014 prints the same
order ([北京中医药大学中医药博物馆, 6 November 2014](https://bowuguan.bucm.edu.cn/kpzl/ysmt/10979.htm)).
Further back it stops matching: the Song-dynasty 《道枢》 version on that page has eight phrases and
no seven bounces at all. A military adaptation in the PLA's newspaper runs the same eight
movements with the punches sixth and holding the feet seventh, adds a three-to-five-second breath
hold to every section and a shout on the punch, and finishes the whole set in about ten minutes
([解放军报, 24 February 2021](http://www.mod.gov.cn/gfbw/gffw/rdjj_214105/4879697.html)).

Seated (文八段) and standing (武八段) families exist, and the drift reaches English. A China
Daily beginner's guide numbers its fifth posture 攒拳怒目增气力 and its seventh 摇头摆尾去心火, with
the descriptions swapped, writes section three as 调理脾胃臂单举 and section eight as 背后七颠诸病消,
and credits the list to a language school rather than to the sport administration
([China Daily, 10 May 2024](https://language.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202405/10/WS663ddf7ca31082fc043c66ec.html)).

The standard moved between draft and release: the April draft's contents page called the first
section 双手托天理三焦, while the registry's section list for the released version says 两手托天理三焦.
The draft carried three annexes — terminology, disease recommendations, resources — and the registry
entry lists four, splitting out the meridian and point diagrams.

## How I checked this

- Form, counts and breathing come from the draft of the standard's technical section, section 5,
  posted for public comment on 30 April 2026
  ([notice](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5402/c29596700/content.html),
  [PDF](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5402/c29596700/part/29596710.pdf),
  [drafting notes](https://www.sport.gov.cn/qgzx/n5402/c29596700/part/29596711.pdf)). The released
  standard's text is not public; what I could read of it is the registry entry.
- The mistake list is translated from the sport administration's correction sheet for the same set; it
  lists errors, no timings.
- The timings are mine: I ran speech recognition over one public run-through labelled
  twelve minutes ([11:59, 719 seconds](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1VsDpYXEqD/)) and read the
  boundaries where the announcer speaks each posture name. A second pass, band-limiting the audio to
  the speech band and marking where the voice starts and stops, agreed within a few seconds at every
  boundary except the two noted above. The column totals to the video's length within a second.
- The twelve-minute figure was checked against four public videos of the same routine, read from their
  pages rather than played.

## What I could not check

- **The final standard.** I read the April 2026 draft for comment; the published T/CACM 1676-2026 is
  dated 28 May 2026 and its text is not public. Every count and breathing instruction here is from
  the draft, and I cannot see whether any of them changed. I could not obtain a later draft.
- **How many annexes the released standard has.** The sports ministry's news report of the release
  says eight chapters and three annexes
  ([中国体育报, 29 May 2026](https://www.sport.gov.cn/n20001280/n20745751/c29657735/content.html)); the
  standards registry lists four. I cannot tell which is right.
- **The per-movement seconds.** One video, two passes, one announcer. The twelve-minute range is the
  platform's own duration field, not a measurement of my own.
- **Whether the recording's repetitions match the standard's.** The recogniser's output is too rough
  to count cue words reliably, and two runs disagree with each other: one undercounts section eight at
  four bounces, the other, seeded with the cue words, catches seven. Where the cues do come through
  they sit near the standard's numbers — three lift-and-drop pairs in section three, three cycles in
  section four — but that is a spot check, not verification. The round counts here come from the
  draft, not from the audio, so if a recording differs, the clock column describes the recording and
  the round column describes the standard.
- **The official demonstration video.** The draft's resource list points to
  [one on the health-qigong association's site](https://www.chqa.org.cn/html/109014-432993.html). I
  did not measure it; the twelve-minute figure rests on public uploads.
- **The closed-breath duration.** The 2017 official article gives about two seconds at the transition
  points; the standard says to hold but gives no duration (`single source`).
- **The effect claims.** Annex B of the draft lists doses and outcomes for 13 conditions, citing
  trials. I did not read those trials and am not repeating their conclusions.
- **The book.** The standard's resource list cites 健身气功·八段锦 (People's Sports Press, 2018
  edition). I have not read it, so nothing here is checked against it.
